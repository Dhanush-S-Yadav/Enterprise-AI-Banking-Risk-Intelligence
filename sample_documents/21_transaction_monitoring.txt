1. Purpose and Applicability
Guide analysts reviewing fictional monitoring alerts without claiming automated fraud detection. This standard is internal to the imaginary Atlas Demo Bank and must not be treated as law, a real bank policy, or advice to customers.

2. Roles and Responsibilities
The AML Monitoring owns the document and maintains an evidence trail. Front-line personnel gather records, reviewers validate conditions, and managers approve documented exceptions according to their role.

3. Required Controls
3.1 Document alert triggers as observed, not proven unlawful behavior.
3.2 Compare event narratives with authorized case evidence and request missing context.
3.3 Maintain an audit trail of case assignment, escalation and closure.
3.4 Do not expose sensitive case information beyond designated roles.

4. Synthetic Case Examples
4.1 A synthetic rapid-transfer alert requires human review of stated context and retained evidence.

5. Evidence and Audit Requirements
For all relevant compliance activities, retain the case identifier, document version, reviewer, date, control result, evidence reference, and any exception rationale. A missing document is an evidence gap; it does not prove control compliance or misconduct.

6. Escalation and Human Judgment
Where a condition is unclear, employees must seek review by the designated owner. The example thresholds are invented for a learning project and cannot be used to decide actual banking services.

7. Limitations
This document is synthetic. It does not represent an actual bank, regulator, statute, customer account, production incident, or live transaction. Any AI-generated summary must cite this source and acknowledge missing evidence.
